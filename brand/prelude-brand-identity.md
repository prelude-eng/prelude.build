## 1. Brand at a glance

**Prelude helps organisations make sound technical decisions and turn them into well-engineered systems.** We combine technology strategy and architecture advisory with the practical ability to design, implement, modernise, and improve software platforms.

Prelude is built on a conviction: **the decisions made before and during implementation shape the systems that follow.** Good strategy must be informed by engineering reality; good engineering must be guided by clear purpose.

We work across software architecture, cloud and platform engineering, systems modernisation, and adjacent areas where our expertise produces a demonstrable benefit. We do not define ourselves by a single programming language, cloud provider, or current technology trend.

### Identity hierarchy

| Layer | Use |

## 2. The name and its meaning

A *prelude* is an opening or introduction that sets the scene for what follows. It reflects the value of **beginnings, preparation, foundations, and possibilities**. Prelude's role is not simply to start projects: it is to help ensure what follows has the right direction and technical foundation.

The name also has a deliberate developer reference. Rust's standard prelude consists of commonly used items automatically brought into scope. The brand expression `prelude::*` alludes to Rust's path and glob-import syntax: the `::*` suffix expresses bringing the accessible items of a module into scope.

This is an **inside reference, not a prerequisite**. Technical audiences may recognise it immediately; nontechnical audiences should still see a concise, distinctive, contemporary engineering brand.

### Meaning translated into brand language

- **Start with intent:** understand the problem, desired outcomes, and constraints.
- **Bring essentials into scope:** focus on the concepts, people, and tools that matter.
- **Engineer for what follows:** make sound decisions that support future change.
- **Move from advice to action:** help deliver the systems, not just describe them.

The name and syntax must not suggest that Prelude works exclusively in Rust.

## 4. Audience and customer problems

### Primary audiences

- **CTOs, VPs of Engineering, and engineering leaders** needing architectural clarity, outside expertise, or delivery capacity.
- **Platform and software teams** tackling reliability, scalability, complexity, operating cost, or system evolution.
- **Product and business leaders** making technology investments that require informed technical evaluation and execution.
- **Founders and growing organisations** seeking experienced architecture guidance without necessarily building a large in-house specialist team immediately.

### Situations that bring clients to Prelude

- A technology decision has significant consequences and competing trade-offs.
- An existing system has become costly, difficult to change, or hard to operate.
- A cloud or platform architecture needs independent review or redesign.
- A team knows the outcome it wants but needs a practical implementation path.
- Strategy exists on paper, but there is a gap between recommendations and delivery.
- New capabilities—including AI where appropriate—must be integrated without sacrificing quality, security, or operational sustainability.

### Desired perception

> “They can see the architectural picture, explain the trade-offs, and help us get the work done.”

## 6. Differentiation and principles

**Advice informed by implementation.** Recommendations are grounded in the realities of building and operating systems.

**Pragmatism over fashion.** We choose technologies to satisfy requirements and constraints, not to follow trends.

**Clarity about trade-offs.** Cost, complexity, performance, maintainability, security, and speed are discussed explicitly.

**Architecture with delivery continuity.** We can help implement the approach we recommend, collaborate with existing teams, and adapt as evidence changes.

**Sustainable outcomes.** Long-term operability and client capability matter as much as initial delivery.

### Five decision principles

1. **Understand before prescribing.** Surface constraints and desired outcomes first.
2. **Keep the essential things in scope.** Focus attention where it changes decisions.
3. **Choose the simplest adequate architecture.** Complexity must earn its place.
4. **Make expertise transferable.** Explain reasoning, document decisions, and collaborate.
5. **Build for the next change.** Design for operational reality and sensible evolution.

## 7. Personality and voice

### Personality

**Technically rigorous · Direct · Curious · Dependable · Pragmatic · Approachable**

Prelude is expert-led, not faceless; confident, not arrogant; technical, not inaccessible.

| Spectrum | Intended position |
|---|---|
| Corporate ↔ Developer-native | Developer-native presentation with enterprise credibility |
| Formal ↔ Conversational | Professional, direct, and human |
| Loud ↔ Restrained | Restrained, with distinctive syntax accents |
| Abstract ↔ Concrete | Concrete, evidence-led, useful |
| Vendor-led ↔ Independent | Independent technical judgement |
| Strategy ↔ Implementation | Explicitly connects both |

### Writing rules

- Lead with the problem or the outcome, not with tool names.
- Prefer specific technical language to broad marketing claims.
- Explain assumptions and trade-offs; say when something is uncertain.
- Use short, confident sentences, but preserve nuance where it matters.
- Avoid gratuitous jargon, hype, and inflated claims.
- Use Irish/British English conventions consistently in general marketing copy (e.g., *organisations*, *modernisation*, *optimisation*), while retaining industry-standard spellings in code and product names.
- Technical jokes and syntax references belong in deliberate, limited contexts.

### Examples

**Avoid:** “We leverage bleeding-edge innovation to revolutionise your digital transformation journey.”

**Prefer:** “We review your architecture, identify the most consequential trade-offs, and help deliver a practical improvement plan.”

**Avoid:** “The best cloud architecture for everyone.”

**Prefer:** “Architecture designed around your workload, team, risk, and operating costs.”

### Message hierarchy

1. **Brand:** Prelude
2. **Brand line:** Systems for what's next.
3. **What we do:** Technology strategy and architecture advisory, with hands-on engineering delivery.
4. **Why it matters:** Sound decisions become dependable, sustainable systems.
5. **Proof:** Clear technical explanations, case studies, outcomes, references, and real work.

## 8. Visual identity direction

### 8.1 Creative direction

**Developer-native, dark-first, monospace-led, code-inspired.**

The identity should feel like a carefully engineered interface: precise, legible, and intentional. It should carry a recognisable developer sensibility without becoming a terminal parody or excluding executive audiences.

**Design keywords:** precise, understated, structural, technically literate, high-contrast, focused, modern.

**Avoid:** decorative circuit boards, random binary digits, fake terminal windows everywhere, overused neon gradients, default cyberpunk styling, and literal Rust mascot or gear artwork.

### 8.2 Primary logotype: `prelude::*`

The primary brand signature is the *literal* expression:

```text
prelude::*
```

**Proposed construction:**

- Lowercase `prelude`, set in a carefully selected monospace or engineered monospaced-inspired type treatment.
- Uninterrupted suffix `::*` placed immediately after the word, respecting authentic code syntax.
- The word rendered in the primary foreground colour; the suffix rendered in the accent colour in standard two-colour applications.
- No unnecessary gradients, glow effects, code brackets, or supplemental emblems.
- Asterisk drawn as an **asterisk**, not automatically replaced by a star, snowflake, compass rose, or spark icon.
- Spacing and glyph forms must be refined as a cohesive logo. Do not assume unmodified typed text is final artwork.

**Important:** `prelude::*` is a graphic signature. In prose and legal documents, use **Prelude** or **Prelude Engineering Limited** as appropriate.

### 8.3 Standalone symbol: `::*`

The compact mark is:

```text
::*
```

The source syntax contains **two colons and one asterisk**. In a typographic icon, each colon has two vertically arranged dots: the symbol therefore has **four dots and a single asterisk**. Preserve that structure faithfully.

Applications include favicon, avatar, slide corner, social profile, sticker, and small interface marks.

**Design requirements:**

- Maintain the punctuation's intended sequence and recognisability.
- Make the dot diameter, colon spacing, asterisk weight, and optical alignment intentional.
- Test at 16, 24, 32, 64, and 128 pixels; simplify only if still recognisably `::*`.
- Supply light-on-dark, dark-on-light, one-colour, and accent-colour versions.
- Prefer an unboxed mark when legible; use a restrained square enclosure for platforms that need a background.

### 8.4 Logo lockups

| Lockup | Intended use |
|---|---|
| `prelude::*` | Primary website, decks, signatures, marketing |
| `prelude::*` + small `ENGINEERING` descriptor | Formal or first-contact contexts needing category clarity |
| `::*` | Favicon, avatars, tiny surfaces, graphics |
| One-colour `prelude::*` | Print, embroidery, limited-colour uses |

An optional descriptor might read **TECHNOLOGY STRATEGY & ENGINEERING** where there is enough space. It is explanatory copy, not part of the core logo.

### 8.5 Typography

**Proposed direction (subject to practical type testing):**

- **Brand/display/code accent:** JetBrains Mono or a comparable high-quality monospace; trial Regular, Medium, and SemiBold.
- **Body/UI:** Inter or an equally clear sans-serif for longer prose, tables, accessibility, and client documents.
- **Technical snippets:** JetBrains Mono with standard code styling; do not overuse syntax highlighting in ordinary marketing copy.

The logo may use custom outlines and optical spacing even if the rest of the identity uses an off-the-shelf font.

**Hierarchy:** bold editorial headlines, concise mono labels, readable body text, generous whitespace, disciplined alignment.

### 8.6 Colour system — proposed

This dark-first palette consolidates the prior explorations; final colours must be tested on real assets.

| Token | Hex | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| `ink` | `#0B0F14` | Primary dark background |
| `surface` | `#111B27` | Elevated dark surfaces |
| `graphite` | `#1F2937` | Borders, panels, supporting UI |
| `cyan` | `#00D1FF` | Syntax accent, focus, selective emphasis |
| `cloud` | `#F8FAFC` | Primary light foreground and light surfaces |
| `slate` | `#94A3B8` | Secondary copy and metadata |

**Usage approach:** dark surfaces dominate; `cloud` provides legible type; cyan is a *signal*, not a blanket decoration. Use it selectively for `::*`, interactive focus, section markers, and meaningful emphasis.

**Light mode:** invert the overall surface/foreground relationship while keeping syntax accents recognisable and sufficiently contrastive. Do not use cyan for small text on white without checking contrast.

**Accessibility gate:** measure WCAG contrast for actual text/background pairs; aim for WCAG 2.2 AA as a baseline. Do not accept a palette purely because the swatches look attractive.

### 8.7 Supporting visual language

- Monospaced annotations, meaningful labels, and restrained rule lines
- Layout grids inspired by technical documentation and thoughtfully composed interfaces
- Precise geometric structures, architecture diagrams, and data visualisation
- Occasional syntax-inspired transitions or reveals tied to content, not ornamental noise
- Carefully placed `::*` motifs, never dense wallpaper
- Strong focus states, reduced-motion support, and clear information hierarchy

**Motion, if used:** minimal, fast, purposeful transitions such as a controlled reveal of the `::*` suffix or a subtle progression from problem to architecture to delivery. Avoid blinking cursors as an always-on decoration.

### 8.8 Imagery

Prefer real engineering work, product interfaces, thoughtful diagrams, architectural structures, and credible technical artefacts. Abstract imagery should have an intelligible relation to the surrounding content.

Avoid generic handshakes, random code screenshots, invented dashboards, and dramatic landscape photography used merely to communicate ambition.

## 9. Digital identity and applications

### Website and domain

**Canonical destination: `https://prelude.build` (planned).**

`prelude.build` reinforces the bridge from prelude to execution: good preparation leads to real systems. Once configured, it should be canonical across all public materials and search metadata.

**Proposed email conventions:** `hello@prelude.build`, `contact@prelude.build`, and individual first-name addresses as needed. These are naming examples, not verified active mailboxes.

### Website structure — initial proposal

1. **Home:** positioning, core problems solved, evidence, contact.
2. **Expertise:** strategy/advisory, architecture, cloud/platform, delivery.
3. **Approach:** how discovery, decisions, delivery, and enablement fit together.
4. **Work:** case studies and clearly attributable outcomes as they become available.
5. **Insights:** detailed engineering writing and technical perspectives.
6. **About:** people, experience, values, and operating model.
7. **Contact:** a low-friction way to start a conversation.

**Homepage hero example:**

> **Systems for what's next.**  
> Technology strategy and architecture advisory, backed by hands-on engineering. We help you make better technical decisions—and turn them into dependable systems.

**Primary CTA:** **Start a conversation**  
**Secondary CTA:** **Explore our expertise**

The website should meet accessibility, performance, security, and SEO basics from the first release. In a developer-native brand, the quality of the site is itself evidence of engineering standards.

### Proposals and technical reports

Use consistent headers, navigation, typographic hierarchy, diagrams, tables, and decision summaries. Make it easy to identify assumptions, findings, trade-offs, recommendations, and next steps. The design should reinforce credibility, not compete with technical material.

### Presentations

Develop title, divider, evidence, comparison, diagram, and conclusion slide templates. Prefer readable technical diagrams to decorative backgrounds; use the primary `prelude::*` logotype sparingly and consistently.

### Developer-facing materials

GitHub, technical posts, examples, conference materials, and stickers can lean further into `prelude::*` and `::*`. They may contain genuine code references, provided the semantics are correct and relevant.

### Social media and avatars

Use `::*` for compact square/circular avatars where the full logotype would become unreadable. Ensure strong contrast and check the artwork at real avatar sizes.

### Legal and administrative communications

Use the company’s full registered name where legally required. The aesthetic brand and syntax mark do not replace statutory details on invoices, contracts, or other regulated documents.

## 11. Governance and consistency

### Do

- Use **Prelude** as the spoken and written name.
- Use `prelude::*` as the primary visual signature.
- Keep the four-dot/two-colon and asterisk structure of `::*` intact.
- Let the language demonstrate practical, independent technical judgement.
- Keep the design system legible, accessible, and deliberate.
- Make strategy and execution equally visible in services and case studies.
- Verify all client claims, metrics, and testimonials before publication.

### Don't

- Present Prelude as exclusively Rust-, AWS-, or AI-focused.
- Replace the asterisk with an unrelated compass or decorative sparkle without a distinct approved concept.
- Use fake terminal output or invented code as visual filler.
- Let dark-mode styling reduce readability.
- Overpromise delivery capacity or imply a larger team than actually exists.
- Confuse domain availability, company registration, and trademark rights.

### Asset production requirements

The eventual production package should include:

- Master logo artwork in SVG, light/dark/monochrome versions
- Standalone `::*` icon in SVG and raster sizes
- Clear-space and minimum-size rules validated by real-size testing
- Accessible colour tokens and typography tokens
- Favicon/browser-icon assets
- Social profile/avatar templates
- Slide/proposal templates
- Website components and usage examples
- Font licence review, IP/trademark clearance, and sign-off record
